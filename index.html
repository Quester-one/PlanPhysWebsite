Towards the multi-turn long-horizon agentic era

The Physics of Multi-Turn Long-Horizon Planning

From pre-training to post-training via single- and multi-teacher on-policy agentic distillation

Tianyi MenChinese Academy of Sciences

Abstract

Where does long-horizon planning come from?

Multi-turn long-horizon planning is a critical ability for foundation-model agents, yet how it improves across training stages remains unclear. We introduce a controlled environment that varies task length, planning difficulty, data quality, planning knowledge, and planning patterns.

Across pre-training, single-teacher post-training, and multi-teacher model merging, our experiments reveal how planning ability is acquired, shaped, and integrated—and where each training strategy reaches its limits.

Three training stages

One capability, three different mechanisms.

The same controlled environment lets us follow planning ability from its origin in pre-training through single- and multi-teacher post-training.

01
Pre-training

Planning begins with an internal world model.

We isolate three data factors—format, distribution, and quality—to trace how long-horizon ability is acquired before post-training.

  • Chain-of-thought state-transition modeling improves long-horizon generalization.
  • Atomic skills do not compose automatically, but a small amount of long-horizon data activates the capability.
  • Suboptimal trajectories are especially harmful because errors accumulate across long sequences.
02
Single-teacher post-training

Planning patterns and knowledge behave differently.

Shared planning behaviors are low-information patterns, while procedures and task-specific solutions are high-information knowledge.

  • OPD has a broader effective boundary than GRPO under low-quality pre-training and long horizons.
  • Dense teacher feedback yields more directionally stable updates than sparse outcome rewards.
  • Distilling unseen multi-path procedures can damage the student’s existing world model.
03
Multi-teacher model merging

Capability transfer depends on shared structure.

MOPD integrates expert policies by finding shared distributions across environments, revealing when merging enables transfer and when it causes interference.

  • Shared, compatible patterns enable cross-environment generalization.
  • Partially shared but conflicting patterns can support continual learning.
  • Fully conflicting patterns with no shared structure cause catastrophic forgetting.
Central takeaway
Long-horizon planning improves when training signals match the structure of the capability being transferred.

World models need the right data distribution. General planning patterns benefit from dense on-policy distillation. Procedural knowledge requires compatible paths. Multi-teacher merging succeeds only when experts share useful structure.

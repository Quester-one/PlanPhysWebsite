Abstract

Long-horizon multi-turn planning remains a fundamental challenge for agentic systems due to the difficulty of maintaining coherent reasoning, adapting to dynamic environments, and executing reliable actions over extended interactions. In this work, we study the physics of multi-turn long-horizon planning and investigate how planning capabilities emerge from pre-training and post-training. We introduce a unified framework based on Single-Teacher and Multi-Teacher On-Policy Agentic Distillation, enabling agents to progressively acquire planning behaviors through interaction-aware supervision.

Overview of our framework.

Overview

We provide a systematic study of multi-turn long-horizon planning. Our analysis reveals that effective planning requires both strong pre-trained representations and efficient post-training alignment mechanisms. The proposed framework decomposes the learning process into:

  • Pre-training for fundamental world modeling and reasoning ability.
  • Single-Teacher On-Policy Agentic Distillation for targeted behavioral refinement.
  • Multi-Teacher On-Policy Agentic Distillation for scalable and diverse planning supervision.
Overall framework of our approach.

Pre-Training

During pre-training, the model learns general knowledge, reasoning patterns, and environmental dynamics. We investigate how different pre-training strategies influence the emergence of long-horizon planning behaviors.

Pre-training pipeline.

Single-Teacher OPD

We introduce Single-Teacher On-Policy Agentic Distillation, where a student agent learns planning behaviors from a single expert teacher through on-policy trajectories. Unlike conventional imitation learning, our approach enables the student to learn from its own generated states, bridging the gap between training and deployment.

Single-Teacher OPD framework.

Multi-Teacher OPD

To improve scalability and robustness, we extend our framework to Multi-Teacher On-Policy Agentic Distillation. Multiple teachers provide complementary planning signals, allowing the student agent to acquire diverse strategies and improve generalization across long-horizon tasks.
